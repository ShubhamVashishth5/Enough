Enough is a home screen and app blocker in one, scored on how little you use your phone, not how much.

<b>A quiet home screen</b>
<ul>
<li>Up to six apps as plain words. No app icons, badges or app grid.</li>
<li>Beside them, up to five small white icons: Phone, Messages and Camera open the phone's own apps, or bind any icon to any app you choose.</li>
<li>Today's top three tasks under the clock, plus your next calendar item. Add more and put them in order, and see what was left undone yesterday.</li>
<li>Battery, next alarm, what's playing, and the weather and air quality, as plain text. Each can be switched off.</li>
<li>A lock button at the bottom right, swipe down for notifications, and an option to hide the status bar.</li>
<li>Your font (mono to start, or the phone's own, light, condensed or serif) and text size, and the size of the clock, app names and icons.</li>
<li>Type to find an app. Distracting apps never auto-launch. Long-press one to uninstall it.</li>
</ul>

<b>The Vault, behind a pause</b>
<ul>
<li>Apps you mark as distracting leave home and search. Opening one starts a breathing pause that grows with each open, then asks what you're there for.</li>
<li>Pick a time box of 2, 5 or 10 minutes. When it ends, you're sent home.</li>
<li>One shared daily budget across every Vault app, so hopping between them doesn't dodge it. Your day starts when you choose: 4 AM unless you change it.</li>
<li>Websites in the Vault get the same rules in Chrome, Firefox, Samsung Internet, Brave and others.</li>
<li>Block just the endless parts: YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels and Explore.</li>
<li>Or make an app a Breathe app: it stays on home, and just pauses for a breath before every open, as long as you set for that app.</li>
</ul>

<b>Commitment</b>
<ul>
<li>Tightening a rule is instant; loosening one waits 24 hours.</li>
<li>Lock every setting for 7, 30 or 90 days.</li>
<li>Skip a wait with a code a friend keeps, by typing a long passage, or with an NFC sticker or printed QR code you leave in another room.</li>
<li>Schedules, a first-hour rule, focus sessions, modes of your own that end when you said they would, wind-down at bedtime and focus sessions from your calendar.</li>
</ul>

<b>Stats that don't become a habit</b>
<ul>
<li>Two numbers on home: Vault minutes and unlocks today.</li>
<li>One weekly report, which opens once a day. CSV export of everything.</li>
</ul>

<b>Private</b>
Usage data never leaves the phone and no account is needed. The only thing that goes online is the optional weather and air quality line, which is off until you switch it on and sends Open-Meteo nothing but a rough location.

<b>Honest limits</b>
Safe mode, a factory reset or a computer with USB debugging can get around everything. The aim is to make cheating take effort, not to make it impossible.

The accessibility service sees which app is in front so the pause screen can appear first, and reads screen content only where a rule you switched on needs it. Without it, Lite protection uses usage access instead: every Vault open is still paused, but feeds and websites aren't covered.

<b>Moving phones</b>
Back up every setting to one file and restore it on the new phone. Anything looser than what's there waits 24 hours, as always.
